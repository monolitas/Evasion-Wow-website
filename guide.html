World systems.

Shared dungeons, open-world PvP, crafting and guild competition. Here’s how the systems of Evasion Worlds work.

Rules and balance can change during closed alpha.

Overview

At a glance.

  • Custom client interfaces. Evasion patches and addons display combat states and custom systems.
  • Visible PvP states. White, purple and red names identify combat status and karma risk.
  • Group and guild play. Parties support hunting; guilds add shared skills, rivalries and territory goals.

01 / The world

Race is not your allegiance.

Alliance and Horde players can group, trade and join the same guild. Rivalries come from players and guilds, not simply your character’s race.

Opposing settlements begin at neutral reputation. Reputation still matters for NPC services and discounts. Cross-faction travel and many faction-wide quests are available; genuine race, class and quest restrictions still apply.

Battleground and arena queues are disabled. Death Knights are unavailable.

02 / Combat

Read the name before you fight.

White · Neutral
Hold Ctrl to deliberately attack. Killing an innocent white player adds karma.
Purple · Flagged
This player has taken part in PvP. Fighting them does not add karma, but it flags you too.
Red · Karma
This player has karma. Attacking them does not add karma; they risk equipment drops on qualifying deaths.

Helping a purple or red player can flag you, including healing a teammate. A flag does not clear just because you relog. Normal PvE area attacks do not accidentally hit innocent players.

Party and raid members are protected from friendly fire. Being in the same guild alone does not give that protection. Settlement guards attack purple and red players; inns protect non-red players outside active siege combat.

Your name colour shows combat state. A blue guild line marks your guild; the green diamond marks your party, or your subgroup in a raid. Siege role icons take priority over clan-war swords.

03 / Consequences

Red means something is at stake.

Killing innocent players increases karma, with a greater cost for repeat PKs. Earned monster experience and qualifying combat deaths reduce it. Red players lose access to ordinary NPC services and inn protection.

A qualifying red-player death can drop worn equipment and carried items. More karma and a higher lifetime PK count increase the risk. Banked items are not part of the drop pool.

Actual drops appear in a public grave. Any living player nearby can open it and take items individually—including the former owner. An empty grave disappears; no drop means no grave.

Guards and eligible monsters can cause red-player drops too. Environmental deaths and weak-monster suicides are not a safe karma-clearing shortcut.

04 / Progression

Death costs experience.

Non-duel deaths apply an experience penalty based on your level. The percentage becomes smaller at higher levels. If you do not have enough current experience, the remainder becomes XP debt.

New experience pays that debt before advancing your level. Direct clan-war deaths receive a reduced penalty; registered siege participants also have a reduced loss. Siege resurrection removes sickness, not the applicable XP penalty.

05 / Exploration

The dungeon belongs to everyone.

Dungeons and raids are shared spaces rather than private copies for each party. You may meet other groups hunting the same creatures, gathering resources or contesting a boss. Evasion PvP rules apply inside too.

Creatures, bosses and gathering nodes have persistent respawn timers. Resetting or re-entering a dungeon does not refill it. Dungeon bosses return on randomized 2–5-hour timers; raid bosses take longer.

Some scripted summoning events use rare summon items. Other encounters keep their normal boss fights. Ordinary treasure chests have reduced equipment drops; materials, gold and crafting remain useful.

06 / Rewards

Contribution decides the loot.

The solo player or group dealing the most effective damage owns a creature’s normal loot, experience and reputation rewards. Pet damage counts for its owner. The winning group uses its selected loot method.

Quest participation is separate. Meaningful damage, tanking or healing can earn quest credit and personal quest items without winning ordinary loot. Nearby party members can share that credit.

Unclaimed ordinary loot and gold become public after a delay. Quest items stay personal. Changing groups does not move damage you already dealt into the new group.

07 / The economy

Equipment can change hands.

Normal equipment is not locked to you by bind-on-pickup or bind-on-equip. Trade it, lend it or sell it after use. Quest-specific binding and normal class, level and inventory restrictions remain.

Engineering makes its equipment and explosives, but non-engineers can use the finished combat items. Profession utilities can still require Engineering. PvP explosives are reduced in power and share a cooldown; high-abuse gadgets are restricted to PvE.

You do not need Engineering on every PvP character. Crafting professions create goods other players can use.

08 / Detection

See the hidden. Don’t remove their stealth.

Engineering Alarm-o-BotAlchemy Scrying PhialJewelcrafting Watcher Prism

These three craftable wards use the same rules. Learn their recipes at profession skill 200. Anyone can use a finished ward.

Choose nearby ground to place one. It lasts two minutes, reveals concealed players within 10 yards and shares a 10-minute cooldown with the other wards. It does not remove stealth or help monsters detect players.

Warnings can occur within 15 yards, but are not guaranteed—and occasional false alarms are part of the quirk. Leaving the reveal radius restores normal concealment. Enemies can Ctrl-attack a ward; three successful hits destroy it without a PK award.

09 / Guild progression

Build strength together.

Completing non-repeatable quests while in a guild earns guild experience. Guild levels grant skill points, which your leader can spend on shared passive skills.

Open Guild → Skills to see bonuses, requirements and costs. Purchases use skill points and the required guild-bank resources. Learned bonuses apply to members; leaving the guild removes them.

Skill purchases are permanent in this version. Clan-war score is separate from guild XP and skill points.

10 / Rivalries

A declared enemy. A deliberate war.

A guild leader challenges another guild through Guild → Clan Wars. War becomes active when the other leader accepts. Both leaders must agree to peace.

Active opponents can attack without Ctrl, except within the same party or raid. War kills award clan points without adding karma or lifetime PKs. Taking hostile action still flags the attacker; ordinary guards and inn rules still matter.

Crossed swords identify an active clan-war enemy. A pending challenge is not an active war.

11 / Territories

Fight for the banner.

Guilds contest configured settlements in hour-long sieges. Speak to the town’s Territory Steward to view ownership, register or manage the town. Registration requires your guild leader to be there; map icons are information only.

Registration & timing

Owners receive seven days of protection and choose a five-hour evening window between 15:00 and midnight. After protection expires, a declaration schedules the next day’s siege. Attackers vote for a start time; voting closes at 23:59 on declaration day and registration closes one hour before battle. Times use London time.

A registered roster is frozen. Switching guilds does not let you change sides. One guild can own only one settlement.

Combat & capture

Everyone inside the siege boundary is flagged, including visitors. Siege combat adds no new karma or PK points; existing red-player drop risks remain. Ordinary town services close.

A registered attacking guild’s leader channels at the banner for 60 seconds. Moving, disabling crowd control, death or logout interrupt capture; damage alone does not. Capture changes guard loyalty and ejects everyone except the new defending guild. Immediate recapture is possible. Whoever controls the banner at the deadline wins.

Death & rally standards

Defenders resurrect in two-minute waves at their siege graveyard. Attacking leaders can place a destructible clan rally standard outside the boundary. Their members’ nearby siege deaths send ghosts there for two-minute waves. Enemy attacks flag the attacker and warn the owning guild.

Without a standard, attackers and uninvolved players who die inside cannot corpse-resurrect inside the siege area. Walk your ghost outside to receive the resurrection prompt. Siege resurrection restores health and mana without sickness; death penalties still apply.

The boundary, compact timer and map tooltip identify the active battle. Being unregistered does not make you safe inside it.

12 / Ownership

Local services fund the town’s owner.

The owning guild’s leader sets a local tax between 0% and 15%, starting at 5%, through the steward. The interface shows the tax and transaction amount.

Purchases & services
Tax is added to local NPC purchases, repairs, training and flights departing town.
Selling to NPCs
Tax is deducted from your sale proceeds. Buyback costs the original gross sale value without another tax.
Not taxed
Player trades, loot and quest rewards.

Collected tax goes into an audited ownership treasury. Leaders withdraw into the guild bank. Losing the town does not erase earnings from the previous ownership.
